Why this name? Step by step
    How to draw
    • Click empty space to place a carbon. Click an atom to add a bond in a zigzag direction.
    • Drag from an atom to draw a bond at 30° steps; drop on another atom to join them.
    • Click a bond to cycle single / double / triple.
    • Pick N, O, F, Cl, Br, I to place or change atoms (a carbon is implied at every corner and end).
    • Shortcuts over an atom: C N O F L(Cl) R(Br) I; over a bond: 1 2 3; Del removes.
    What this prototype covers
    • Up to 20 carbons; parent chain or ring up to 10 carbons.
    • Alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, single carbocycles.
    • Alkyl, alkenyl and cycloalkyl branches of any complexity (e.g. 2-methylpropyl).
    • Halogens, alcohols, ethers (as alkoxy), aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters (e.g. methyl ethanoate), acyl halides (e.g. ethanoyl chloride), primary / secondary / tertiary amines (e.g. N,N-dimethylethanamine), amides (e.g. N-methylpropanamide) and nitriles (e.g. propanenitrile).
    • Parent selection follows the classic school rules: most principal groups, then the ring or chain with more carbons (ring wins a tie), then most C=C / C≡C, then longest chain, then most substituents, then lowest locants.
    • Not yet: benzene/aromatics, fused or bridged rings, N-heterocycles, imines, nitro compounds, anhydrides, lactones, stereochemistry (E/Z, R/S).
