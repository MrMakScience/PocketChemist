Try it on paper first

  1. Choose which formula you want to be given.
  2. Draw or write the other two on paper.
  3. Press Show on each to check, then point at or tap an atom to match it across the formulas.
Start from

Condensed formula

  • Write each carbon with its hydrogens: CH₃, CH₂, CH.
  • Put branches in brackets straight after the carbon they hang from: CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃.
  • Show double and triple bonds with = and ≡.
  • Groups are written as units: OH, CHO, COOH, CO, COO.

Skeletal formula

  • Every corner and every line end is a carbon atom.
  • Hydrogens on carbon are not drawn. Count them: each carbon needs four bonds.
  • Atoms other than C and H are written out, with their own hydrogens: OH, Cl, O.
  • Double bonds are two lines, triple bonds three lines.

Displayed formula

  • Draw every atom and every bond, including each C–H bond.
  • Each carbon has exactly four bonds, oxygen two, hydrogen and halogens one.
  • Angles are drawn at 90° for clarity. They do not show the real 3D shape.
  • An O–H group is drawn with its bond to H.

Check your working

  • Count the bonds on every carbon. Four means it is complete.
  • Molecular formula the same? Count the carbons first, then the hydrogens.
  • Same formula but a different drawing is an isomer: look at where the branch or group is attached.
