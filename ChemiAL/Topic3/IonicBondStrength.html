What the symbols mean

The small + or − beside a letter only tells you which ion is meant: + is the cation, − is the anion. It is a label, not a power.

q⁺
Charge of the cation (the positive ion), as a plain number. Mg²⁺ has q⁺ = 2.
q⁻
Charge of the anion (the negative ion), as a plain number with the minus sign ignored. O²⁻ has q⁻ = 2.
r⁺
Ionic radius of the cation.
r⁻
Ionic radius of the anion.
d
Distance between the two nuclei. In the solid the ions touch, so d = r⁺ + r⁻.

Model used: strength is proportional to (q⁺ × q⁻) ÷ d. Values are relative, so they have no unit. Melting points are approximate experimental values, shown to compare with the trend. Melting point also depends on lattice structure, so use it as supporting evidence only.

Predict, then test

  1. Charge only. Start from Na⁺ Cl⁻. Predict what happens when both charges go from 1 to 2 with the radii unchanged, then move the sliders. By what factor does the strength change?
  2. Radius only. Keep both charges at 1. Compare Li⁺ F⁻ with Cs⁺ I⁻. Which pair has the shorter distance between nuclei, and which has the stronger bonding?
  3. Two effects together. Press “Use current setting as reference” on Na⁺ Cl⁻, then choose Mg²⁺ O²⁻. Explain how both the charge and the radius contribute to the difference.
