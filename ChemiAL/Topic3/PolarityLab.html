Bonding and polarity

Electron tug of war

Every covalent bond is a rope with a pair of electrons knotted on it. The atom with the higher electronegativity pulls harder. Whether a whole molecule ends up polar depends on one thing: do its ropes cancel out, or add up?

Adding the pulls

Each bond dipole is an arrow whose length is its ΔEN. Place them head to tail, one after another, in the same 3D directions as the bonds. Drag the view to turn it, or use the arrow keys, and look at the loop from several sides.

Back at the start? The pulls cancel. The net dipole is zero and the molecule is non-polar.

Ended somewhere else? The purple arrow from start to finish is the net pull. The molecule is polar, and the δ− end is where that arrow points.

Electronegativity values are Pauling values, rounded to one decimal place. Rule of thumb used here: ΔEN below 0.4 is treated as non-polar, 0.4 to 1.8 as polar covalent, and above 1.8 as mostly ionic. Real bonds sit on a continuum, so the cut-offs are a convenience.
