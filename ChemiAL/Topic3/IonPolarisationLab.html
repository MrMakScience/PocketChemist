Increasing polarisation

The anion keeps the same total amount of electron cloud: whatever the neck takes comes out of the body, so the body shrinks inside the dashed circle. Radii are typical ionic radii in picometres (pm); check them against your data booklet. The 0 to 100 polarization degree is a teaching scale built from charge density and anion size. Use it to compare trends, not to classify a compound: real bonding also depends on factors such as electronegativity and lattice energy.

Two factors to remember

The polarising power of a cation and the polarisability of an anion depend on the radius of the ion and the charge of the ions.

Polarising power of cations

  • A highly charged cation has a stronger electrostatic attraction with an anion, which distorts the anion's electron cloud.
  • A cation with a smaller radius generally concentrates its charge in a small area, so the electron cloud is distorted more.
  • The polarising power increases with an increase in charge density and a decrease in radius.

Polarisability of anions

  • The more negatively charged an anion is, the greater its electron cloud.
  • The larger the electron cloud, the more easily it is distorted.
  • A larger radius means the outer electrons are held more loosely, so they are more easily distorted.
  • The anion's polarisability increases as the radius increases and the charge becomes more negative.

Same anion, stronger cation

Na⁺, Mg²⁺ and Al³⁺ each have 10 electrons, so the extra nuclear charge pulls the outer electrons in and the radius falls. Each is paired here with the same O²⁻ ion.

Polarisation can give covalent character to ionic bonding. The larger the distortion, the more covalent character.

Try it and predict

1. Start at Mg²⁺ + Cl⁻ and raise the cation charge to 3+. What changes?

Keep the radius at 72 pm and move the cation charge slider from 2+ to 3+. Predict first, then check the diagram and the polarization degree.

The charge density rises by half (3 ÷ 2), so polarising power rises. The neck grows longer and the body of the cloud shrinks a little more.

2. Hold the cation at 2+ and shrink its radius from 100 pm to 60 pm.

Watch the charge density readout as you slide.

Charge density rises by (100 ÷ 60)², about 2.8 times. The same charge sits in a smaller area, so the distortion is greater.

3. Click Al³⁺ + F⁻, then Al³⁺ + Cl⁻, then Al³⁺ + I⁻. What is the trend?

The cation stays the same. Only the anion changes.

Covalent character increases down the group. F⁻ is small, so its electrons are held tightly and AlF₃ stays largely ionic. Cl⁻ and I⁻ are larger, their outer electrons are held more loosely, and the same Al³⁺ ion distorts them much more.
