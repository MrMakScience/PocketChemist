What is concentration?

Reactions like to happen in solution

In a solid, the particles are locked in place. Only the ones on the surface can meet the other chemical. When a substance dissolves, its particles spread out and move around freely, so they collide with the other reactant everywhere in the liquid. That makes many reactions much faster.

  • Silver nitrate and sodium chlorideMix the two dry white solids and nothing obvious happens. Dissolve each in water and mix them: a white precipitate of silver chloride forms straight away.
  • Reactions in your bodyCells and blood are mostly water. Medicines and nutrients are carried dissolved in body fluids, so the chemistry of life happens in solution.

The catch: a solution only works well at the right concentration. Too weak and little happens. Too strong and it wastes chemicals, or becomes dangerous.

Concentration: how much solute in how much solution

Solute

The substance that dissolves, such as salt.

Solvent

The liquid that does the dissolving, usually water.

Solution

The solute and solvent mixed together.

Remember: 1000 cm³ = 1 dm³ = 1 L and M = mol dm−3

Why the right concentration matters: a real medicine

Paracetamol (also called acetaminophen) relieves pain and fever when its concentration in the blood is about 10 to 20 mg/L. Below that it may not work. Far above it, it can damage the liver, and doctors use about 150 mg/L, four hours after a dose, as the level where emergency treatment is needed.

The dose has to suit the person. A medicine spreads through body fluid, roughly 0.9 L for every kg of body mass, so a smaller person has less fluid to dilute the same dose. That is why children's doses are worked out from body mass.

This is a simplified model for learning. Real doses come from the label, a doctor or a pharmacist, who also allow for how fast the body removes the drug. If anyone takes too much medicine, contact emergency services or a poison information centre straight away.

Dilution of concentration

Water changes the volume, not the moles

When you dilute a solution you add water. The water carries no solute, so the moles of solute stay fixed. Only the volume grows, so the concentration falls.
