Finding molar mass from a gas volume

Why the empirical formula is not enough

We have learnt to find an empirical formula. It only gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. CH2O could be methanal, ethanoic acid or glucose, and those are three very different molecules.

To find the true molecular formula we also need the molar mass, M, of the compound.

Many compounds behave like an ideal gas once they are vaporised. That lets us use the gas equation to weigh their molecules.

Turn pV = nRT into a formula for M

We cannot count moles directly, but we can weigh the sample. The number of moles is n = m ÷ M. Swap that in and rearrange.

  1. pV = nRT the ideal gas equation
  2. n = m ÷ M moles from mass and molar mass
  3. pV = (m ÷ M) × RT replace n
  4. M = m × R × Tp × V multiply by M, divide by pV

Same ratio, different molecules

These three molecules have different structures and different sizes. Count the atoms in each one: they are all whole-number multiples of one carbon, two hydrogen and one oxygen. That makes the empirical formula CH2O for all of them. Only the molar mass can tell them apart.

The experiment

A small, weighed amount of a volatile liquid is injected through a rubber cap into a gas syringe. The syringe is kept hot, above the boiling point of the liquid. The liquid vaporises and pushes the plunger out until the gas pressure equals the air pressure. You then read three things: the volume on the barrel, the temperature of the heating and the pressure of the room. The mass is the mass you injected.

Try the experiment

Choose an unknown liquid, set the mass you inject, the heating temperature and the room pressure, and watch the plunger. The liquids have a hidden molar mass. Read the volume, then press Work out the molar mass to see the method.

    Where do 103 and 106 come from?

    • 1 m = 10 dm so 1 m³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 dm³
    • 1 dm = 10 cm so 1 dm³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 cm³
    • 1 m³ = 1000 × 1000 cm³ = 10⁶ cm³ the same as (100 cm)³

    From composition and gas data to molecular formula

    The plan, in five stages

    1. Table methodMass, RAM, moles, divide by the smallest, make whole numbers.
    2. Empirical formulaWrite it and work out its mass, Mr.
    3. Molar mass of the gasConvert to SI, then M = mRT ÷ pV.
    4. Find nn = M ÷ Mr of the empirical formula. Round to a whole number.
    5. Molecular formulaMultiply every subscript in the empirical formula by n.
    Two different whole numbers appear in these questions. A multiplier such as × 2 in the ratio table (to turn 2.5 into 5) belongs to the empirical formula. The number n at the end belongs to the molecular formula. Do not mix them up.

    Relative atomic masses (RAM) and R are given in every question. Percentages become grams if you pretend the sample is 100 g. The unit rules from lesson 1 still apply: kPa to Pa, cm3 and dm3 to m3, °C to K, mg to g.
