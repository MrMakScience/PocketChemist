Fractionating Tower Simulator

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons. Heat it, let the vapour rise up a column that gets cooler towards the top, and each fraction condenses where the temperature drops below its boiling point.

    How the separation works

    1. Crude oil is an even mixture of hydrocarbons of all chain lengths. It is pumped through a tube into a furnace and heated. The furnace heats it to 450 °C. Molecules that can vaporise enter the tower as vapour. The largest molecules cannot vaporise, so they sink to the bottom as residue.
    2. The tower is hottest at the bottom, where the hot feed from the furnace enters. The higher up the column, the further it is from this heat source, and the rising vapour loses heat as it goes, so the temperature decreases. This is the temperature gradient.
    3. Vapours rise. Each one condenses on the tray at the height where the temperature falls below its boiling point.
    4. The liquid is drawn off from that tray. Shorter chains with lower boiling points condense higher up. Very short chains never condense and leave as gases.
